# Future Educational Impact Study Design

This document is a proposed evaluation design, not a report of a completed study.

## Research question

Does an adaptive, course-grounded tutor produce greater short-session learning gains than a comparison learning experience using the same course materials?

## Pilot design

```text
Recruit eligible learners
        ↓
Consent and baseline questionnaire
        ↓
Pre-test on target concepts
        ↓
Random assignment
   ↙                 ↘
Control            Treatment
standard reading   EduAgent session
or non-adaptive    with retrieval,
course chatbot     quizzes, and policy
        ↘                 ↙
Post-test on parallel items
        ↓
Survey, completion time, and analysis
```

## Primary outcome

```text
Learning gain = Post-test score − Pre-test score
```

The analysis should report group sizes, uncertainty, attrition, item difficulty, and the exact scoring procedure. Do not report only a favorable mean difference.

## Secondary outcomes

- Quiz performance during the session
- Completion time
- Number of concepts practiced
- Perceived helpfulness and cognitive load
- Citation usefulness and trust
- Qualitative misconceptions or failure cases

## Comparison conditions

The control must be specified before data collection. Possible controls include reading the same material, a non-adaptive RAG chatbot, or a static quiz workflow. The treatment and control should receive comparable content exposure and time limits.

## Ethics and privacy

- Obtain institutional review or exemption determination as required.
- Obtain informed consent and make participation voluntary.
- Collect the minimum personal data needed.
- Separate study identifiers from raw responses.
- Define retention, deletion, and access procedures before recruitment.
- Avoid using automated feedback as an official grade.
- Allow participants to stop without penalty.
- Test accessibility and provide a human contact for concerns.

## Limitations

A short session may measure immediate recall rather than durable learning. Randomization may be underpowered in a small pilot. Participants may differ in prior knowledge, motivation, and familiarity with AI tools. LLM feedback can vary across runs, and source retrieval quality may confound the intervention. These limitations should be reported rather than hidden.
